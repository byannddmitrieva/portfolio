A visual identity refresh for one of Russia’s largest real-estate developers.

ART DIRECTOR — ANNA DMITRIEVA / SAMOLET
DESIGN — SAMOLET IN-HOUSE TEAM
SELECTED BRAND UPDATES — WITH ESH AND PRAGMATICA

A visual identity refresh.

We refreshed Samolet’s visual identity around a simple idea: home brings people, everyday experiences and emotions together. The aim was a more cohesive style with a warmer, more human tone.

As art director at Samolet, I worked with the in-house team on visual direction and its rollout across campaigns, digital, print and physical environments.

Key visuals. Type. Icons.

We updated the key visual system, introduced an italic typographic accent alongside the existing sans-serif, and refreshed the iconography. Together, these changes added warmth and expression while keeping the communication clear and consistent across formats.

A warmer visual world.

The updated key visuals pair everyday scenes with soft 3D forms and familiar objects. A shared blue palette connects the imagery, bringing the idea of home into the brand’s communication.

One language.
Across the city.

The visual system scales up to outdoor campaigns. Clear messages, expressive imagery and a recognisable blue palette connect billboards, street formats and neighbourhood communication.

Digital touchpoints.

The same language adapts to the company website and social media: from a clear information hierarchy to expressive stories and campaign messages.

Stories in motion.

Brand films and AI motion studies carry the visual language into stories about home, family and everyday life. The format changes; the brand’s character stays connected.

From screen to print.

A shared graphic language connects digital communication with printed materials. Brochures and sticker sheets bring together typography, icons and tactile forms in a more personal format.

A brand you can inhabit.

Wayfinding, residential interiors and sales offices bring the identity into physical space. The graphics help the brand remain recognisable as people move through different environments.

Small details.
Everyday care.

The brand also appears in the details of neighbourhood life: recycling points, pet-care stations, signs for planted areas and small seasonal gestures. Practical messages share the same friendly tone.

From symbols to objects.

The visual language extends into three dimensions: interior objects, soft merchandise, gift sets and clothing. Familiar brand shapes become things people can encounter, use and keep.

People bring
the brand to life.

Events and exhibition spaces bring the system together at a human scale. Environmental graphics, printed details and small takeaways create a connected brand experience.

One identity.
Many everyday moments.

The refreshed visual language carries one idea of home across different scales — from a small sticker or digital story to a neighbourhood, a campaign or a shared event.
